Two days, captured a couple of weeks back, stand in as well as any others could. Same shape, different result.

Day one starts at 8, later than I wanted. Tai Chi, still deciding if it earns a permanent spot, then the treadmill, then coffee. By the time I sit down at the desk, there are already three separate things waiting, not one.

Thirty leads in the job pipeline. Filtered to twelve, then down to six worth applying for. Six is a good number — enough to write a targeted resume and cover letter for each one instead of spraying the same document at everything. That alone is a morning.

Then the knowledge base work starts, because it doesn't wait for the job search to finish first. Tech newsletters, LinkedIn posts, anything worth keeping, fed into a system I'm building for later — future articles, consulting material, whatever it ends up being. Comments on other people's posts, insights logged. The publishing pipeline gets its own pass on top of that — drafting, reviewing, structuring new ideas so they don't just evaporate before I get back to them.

Lunch. TV. Waiting on AI usage limits to reset, because even the tools have a ceiling some days.

Afternoon is roadmap work — not writing, deciding. Prioritizing the personal management system build over everything else that's open, prepping for the consulting launch, structuring the rest so it can wait its turn without falling apart in the meantime. An errand, dinner with my wife, the news. Then back to the office, because six applications aren't going to submit themselves.

This is the part worth saying plainly: application portals are the worst part of a job search. Re-entering the same resume information by hand into a form that already has your resume attached to it. No field for a cover letter half the time. I hit two portals from companies I'd have expected better from, and it didn't leave a great impression — if you're on the hiring side and want to know what candidate experience actually feels like, that's it. That's its own post, not this one.

Comments, messages, some TV, cards scanned for the collection site. That's day one — four separate workstreams, none of them finished, all of them moved forward.

Day two starts later — which, it turns out, is fine. It pushes the workout later too, and that's going better than my last attempt at building a habit did. Only four leads in the pipeline this time, one worth pursuing. Smaller day on that front. Not a smaller day overall.

The content review runs the same as always — email, LinkedIn, the usual sweep. Finalized the next long-form Canada AI piece and drafted the one after it. A week ahead on content now, which feels like the first real cushion I've had, and it doesn't happen by accident — it happens because I don't let a light job-search day turn into an empty day.

Lunch, a pharmacy run, then the actual centerpiece: the BRD for the management system. I used Claude to pull a first draft from a pile of scattered sources — old conversations, learning logs from other processes I'd already built. It got to a solid starting point fast. Getting from "solid start" to "actually correct" was still on me — missed features, fuzzy logic, specifics that only I knew. Worth saying: building learning logs into every prototype process from the start makes this kind of thing much faster later. I'd skip that step at my own cost.

Reference pages went into Notion for the automated processes — editable, so the system can change without touching code. BRD finalized, mockup session added to the plan, every open decision closed out. Next step will be technical design. That's most people's whole day, done before dinner.

Then dinner, and a last-minute decision to spend the evening on Canada Day personal site content instead — a personal post and a collecting post, both with their own images. Writing wasn't the slow part. The images were. Nothing about that was on the schedule that morning, and it still got done alongside everything else that was.

Seven applications between the two days, all with tailored resumes and cover letters, not copy-paste. A finished BRD, a week of content banked, two Canada Day posts built after dinner. Call it unemployed if you want. It's a full day's work. It just doesn't come with a business card.
