![Hawkeye](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/header-hawkeye.jpg "Hawkeye")

*Not my first favorite superhero. Not even my first comic. That was GI Joe, and before Avengers, the closest thing I had to a superhero book as a kid was Teen Titans.*

Avengers became my book when GI Joe wasn't enough reading anymore. I had a subscription and a standing trip to the comic store, and when Joe was winding down I started filling the space with other things. Avengers stuck, and it became my favorite superhero title outright.

Team books were the reason. GI Joe was a team book too, and that connects to something about me I never noticed as a kid: I'm a team person. Team sports, not individual ones. Comfortable working in a group. Uncomfortable trying to find one. I liked to belong.

That's probably also why Hawkeye. He's not the team captain or the MVP - what's the guy with the bow going to do that the billionaire in the suit of armor can't? I was happy to belong on the team, and I think, without realizing it at the time, I felt the same way about him. I just wished I had his confidence.

---

Comic collecting means want lists, and want lists mean comic conventions. My Avengers reading started partway through the West Coast Avengers run, which made it easy to find in back-issue bins - along with Solo Avengers and Avengers Spotlight, which meant a lot of Hawkeye stories early on. He stuck. I drifted into X-Men for a while, then hard into Wildstorm at Image - not Spawn like everyone else, Wildstorm did team books well, with better art - but I always came back to Avengers and Hawkeye.

Every solo series that came out was a must-have. The idea of an Avengers movie was already huge for anyone who grew up on this stuff. It was bigger knowing Hawkeye would be in it. Most superhero fans had already had their Superman, Spider-Man, Batman, and X-Men movies. This was my first ride with my guy. And Renner was the right choice - that's not really up for debate in my house.

![Jeremy Renner autograph card](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/autograph-jeremy-renner.jpeg "Jeremy Renner as Hawkeye, Upper Deck Signature Sensations")
![Hailee Steinfeld autograph card](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/autograph-hailee-steinfeld.jpeg "Hailee Steinfeld as Kate Bishop, Upper Deck Signature Sensations")

Then Kate Bishop showed up, and she's still Hawkeye to me. I didn't buy the Young Avengers issues where she first appeared - wasn't paying attention to the peripheral titles back then - but when a new Hawkeye book was announced, I added it to my subscription before I even realized it wasn't the same character. No disappointment. Now I had two Hawkeyes to collect, and an origin run to add to the want list.

## What's actually in the collection

Hawkeye is a tier 1 collection for me, which means I collect anything with the character or the logo on it - especially the logo, I love a good logo. Comics, toys, non-sport cards, Funko Pops, badges, buttons, t-shirts, hats, statues. On the comics side: every Hawkeye series, plus anything with him on the cover. At a con, my artist auto list always has a Hawkeye book on it, and Boba Fett, and GI Joe, but that's a different post.

![Hawkeye shelf](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/shelf-group-shot.jpeg "Sketch cover, figure, and the Upper Deck Hawkeye box together")

The whale in any comic character PC is the first appearance. Tales of Suspense #57, September 1964. I've got a raw one, but this is even more special - a CGC 4.0 copy signed by Stan Lee. Maybe I go for a higher grade some day, but for now, owning it is more than enough.

![Tales of Suspense #57, CGC 4.0, signed by Stan Lee](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/tales-of-suspense-57.jpeg "Tales of Suspense #57 - origin and first appearance of Hawkeye, signed by Stan Lee")

If anyone knows where I can get the sky-cycle statue, I still regret not buying it when I had the chance.

![Hawkeye on Sky-Cycle statue box](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/sky-cycle-statue-gentle-giant.png "Gentle Giant's Hawkeye on Sky-Cycle, 12-inch limited edition statue - product photo, not my own piece")

![Original color commission of Hawkeye and Kate Bishop](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/commission-color-siya-oum.jpg "Hawkeye and Kate Bishop, commission by Siya Oum, 2025")

Then there's the art. Flipping through OA binders, I'm always looking for a Hawkeye panel. Getting a commission, one of the two Hawkeyes is always on the shortlist. My OA collection only holds a handful of covers, and three of them are Hawkeye books. My higher-end commissions skew heavily toward the Avenging Archer.

I want color in commissions. Black and white works fine for OA, but a commission should have that splash of color. Purple is my color, and that's not a coincidence - Hawkeye pushed me there, not the other way around. Twenty years ago I'd have said blue.

## The collection in data, just how I like it

![Hawkeye across the collection, item counts by category](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/hawkeye-footprint.png "Hawkeye across the collection")

![Hawkeye comics breakdown, 246 total](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/hawkeye-comics-breakdown.png "Hawkeye comics - 246 total")

![Hawkeye card types, 317 total](images/2026-07-18-core-pc1-hawkeye/hawkeye-card-types.png "Hawkeye cards - 317 total")

Comics lead the count at 246 - 139 true Hawkeye issues, 55 cover appearances in other people's books, 33 related titles, and 19 signed copies. Cards come in just ahead at 317, with base and parallel making up nearly two-thirds of that. Of those, 162 are tagged to Clint Barton and 69 to Kate Bishop as the featured character - the rest are set and team cards that don't call out either by name. The sketch cards and autographs are where the collection gets more personal - including cards signed by Jeremy Renner and Hailee Steinfeld.

The everything-else side tells a different story. Art - sketch covers and commissions together - makes up 40 of the 93 items, more than any other category. That tracks. If I'm commissioning something, odds are good it's got a bow in it.
