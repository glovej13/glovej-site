People talk a lot about what drives them. What they're good at, what fires them up, what they're chasing. Less gets said about the other half: what actually lets you stop. Both matter. A person who can't switch off eventually runs the same way an engine does with no idle setting, and I've spent enough of the last few years finding that out the hard way.

A few things shut my brain off completely. Not distract it. Shut it off. No spare capacity left for anything else.

Reading fiction is one. Three books in six days on a trip a while back, and it wasn't really about the plot. It was building the world in my head. Hearing the cast deliver lines I hadn't even finished writing yet in my imagination. That takes the whole machine. There's nothing left over to worry with.

Certain TV shows do the same thing, but only if I watch them properly. Half-watching with a phone in hand doesn't work. I miss something and the whole thing falls apart. The shows that actually blank me out are the ones that punish multitasking. If a show survives me checking email during a scene, it isn't doing the job.

Coaching hockey is the strongest version of it. During a tournament I'm not thinking about anything except line combinations and what happens if we lose the next game. The logistics, hotel bookings, meal schedules, who's driving who, got handled by other people. That wasn't a plan I made. I didn't delegate it on purpose. My brain didn't have a slot open for it, so somebody else had to pick it up, because I genuinely could not.

That's the pattern. Not distraction in the scroll-your-phone sense. The opposite. Something demanding enough to fill every available slot, so nothing else gets through the door. Not the news. Not the thing from work still sitting unresolved. Not whatever the background hum of worry happens to be that week.

I've started noticing when it isn't there. Some weeks I go looking for it and it doesn't show up. I pick up the book and I'm still half in my own head. The game doesn't have the usual pull. That's a different problem, and a different post.
