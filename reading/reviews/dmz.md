## DMZ
Brian Wood (writer), Riccardo Burchielli (artist) — Graphic Novel / Political Fiction

72 issues, 2005–2012, collected into 12 volumes — Manhattan as the demilitarized zone of a second American civil war.

DMZ ran on Vertigo for seven years and built its reputation on being uncomfortably plausible rather than purely dystopian spectacle. Wood drew on his years living in New York, in the shadow of 9/11, to ground the series in a version of the city readers would actually recognize — Central Park, the Flatiron Building, the geography of a real place, repurposed under different rules. Critics generally credit it as one of the sharper political comics of its era for exactly that reason: it doesn't ask you to imagine an alien world, it asks you to imagine your own city under occupation.

Read this one a while back — the full run. The thing about a series like this is you're not really judging it on the strength of the art or the pacing alone. You're judging whether it earns the dystopia. Any future-dystopia story lives or dies on whether the reader can trace a line from here to there. DMZ drew that line, using a version of Manhattan close enough to the real thing that the premise never felt like a stretch. What felt like speculative fiction when it was written reads a lot less far-fetched now, and that's the whole test it had to pass.
