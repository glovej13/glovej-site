*The Queer Eye culture expert's memoir, and the book behind a specific pull I still haven't fully written about.*

I came to this one as a fan of the show, the way most people probably do. What I got was a much wider story: a Jamaican-Cuban upbringing in the South, The Real World in 2004 as the first openly gay Black man on the cast, addiction, a long road to fatherhood, and a real argument for what "culture" actually means that goes well past the Queer Eye makeover format.

That last part is the throughline. Brown redefines culture as something closer to how people feel about themselves and each other, not museums or ballet, and once you see the show through that lens the whole "culture expert" title stops sounding like a stretch. "I am culture," he says early on, and the rest of the book is him making the case for it.

It's a lighter entry on this list than some of the others, and I'll be honest about why: I read it a while ago, before I was tracking notes the way I do now, so this is a lighter pass than a full reread deserves. What I remember clearly is how the book kept circling back to the idea that healing requires the hard conversation, not avoidance of it.
