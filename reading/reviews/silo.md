## Silo
Hugh Howey — Dystopian Science Fiction

- *Wool* (2012)
- *Shift* (2013)
- *Dust* (2013)

Howey originally self-published *Wool* as a short story in 2011 before it grew into a full trilogy, and the series became one of the more notable indie-to-mainstream success stories in science fiction — enough that Howey turned down seven-figure publishing offers to keep control of the digital rights. *Shift* works as a prequel, explaining how the silos came to exist, before *Dust* pulls both books together for the ending. It's now the basis for the Apple TV+ series of the same name. General reception has been strong throughout, with reviewers consistently singling out the world-building — the silo itself functions almost as a character — as the trilogy's strongest asset, with the middle book's flashback structure being the most divisive point among readers.

Most recent read on this list, and the fastest — the full trilogy in three days. A book has to be doing something right to hold that kind of focus.

Another strong entry in the future-dystopia category, and another one built on world-building rather than spectacle. I hadn't seen the Apple TV+ show going in, so I got to piece together the silo's history and all its interlocking systems the way Howey intended — one layer at a time, as the story expanded outward. That's the best way to read it, honestly.
